## Introduction
This document evaluates container orchestration solutions suitable for teams managing small-scale applications (5-20 services) in 2026. As organizations look to balance complexity, cost, and operational overhead, we've synthesized insights on Kubernetes and its alternatives.

## Kubernetes Overview
### Advantages
- **Scalability**: Kubernetes is designed to manage complex, distributed systems and supports scaling applications seamlessly.
- **Ecosystem**: A robust ecosystem with numerous integrations, plugins, and tools.

### Disadvantages
- **Operational Overhead**: High complexity in setup and ongoing management. Requires continuous monitoring, resource optimization, and life cycle management.
- **Learning Curve**: Steep learning curve for teams new to container orchestration.

### Ongoing Work Requirements
- **Lifecycle Management**: Upgrading clusters, maintaining consistency, and managing configurations.
- **Resource Monitoring**: Continual oversight of resource usage, implementing autoscaling, and optimizing performance.
- **Security Compliance**: Regular audits and updates of security policies and configurations.
- **Networking Management**: Configuring service discovery, load balancing, and maintaining network configurations.

## Alternatives to Kubernetes
1. **Amazon ECS (Elastic Container Service)**
   - **Usage**: Best for teams within the AWS ecosystem.
   - **Reasons for Choice**: 
     - Low operational burden and direct integration with AWS services.
     - Task definitions simplify container runtime management.

2. **Google Cloud Run**
   - **Usage**: Ideal for stateless applications and quick deployments.
   - **Reasons for Choice**:
     - Serverless capabilities reduce management overhead.
     - Focused on rapid application deployment without orchestration complexity.

3. **HashiCorp Nomad**
   - **Usage**: Suitable for teams needing lightweight orchestration.
   - **Reasons for Choice**:
     - Simplified management of both containers and VMs with less complexity than Kubernetes.
     - Integrates well with service discovery and secrets management (Consul and Vault).

4. **Docker Swarm**
   - **Usage**: Best for existing Docker users.
   - **Reasons for Choice**:
     - Seamlessly extends Docker capabilities, using familiar Docker Compose syntax.
     - Rapid deployment and low-friction path to production.

5. **OpenShift**
   - **Usage**: Enterprises requiring enhanced governance and security features.
   - **Reasons for Choice**:
     - Additional management tools and vendor support for compliance needs.

6. **Civo Kubernetes**
   - **Usage**: For small to medium deployments seeking simplicity.
   - **Reasons for Choice**:
     - Streamlined management and reduced setup time tailored for Kubernetes environments.

## Cost Implications
**Managed Kubernetes Services**: Higher ongoing costs due to management fees but lower operational overhead when compared to self-hosted solutions. Expect monthly expenses ranging from $100 to $300 for smaller setups.

**Self-Hosted Kubernetes**: Typically cheaper at first glance, but operational complexities can lead to hidden costs (e.g., staffing, maintenance tools). Typical total costs are around €80,000–€120,000 annually for a medium-sized cluster.

**Alternatives**: 
- Amazon ECS and Google Cloud Run often present lower operational burdens and costs suitable for small projects.
- Docker Swarm may reduce costs significantly due to minimal resource requirements.

## Conclusion
For teams managing small-scale applications in 2026, the choice of container orchestration should be guided by specific project needs, team expertise, and existing infrastructure. Kubernetes remains a powerful but complex option, while alternatives like Amazon ECS, Google Cloud Run, HashiCorp Nomad, and Docker Swarm provide simpler solutions with varying benefits. Selecting the right platform will hinge on evaluating the team's capacity for managing operational tasks, cost considerations, and the specific requirements of the applications being deployed.

## 1. Team Readiness
Before adopting any container orchestration solution, especially Kubernetes or its alternatives, teams should assess their readiness in the following areas:

### Technical Knowledge
- **Kubernetes**: Teams need a strong understanding of container orchestration concepts, networking, and services.
- **Alternatives**: Depending on the chosen solution, familiarity with specific tools like AWS services, Docker commands, or HashiCorp tools may be necessary.

### Infrastructure Readiness
- Teams should ensure their underlying infrastructure (cloud or on-premises) is capable of supporting the chosen orchestration platform, including network and storage configurations.

### DevOps Practices
- Established CI/CD processes and automation practices are essential for deploying and managing containers effectively.

### Team Capability
- Identify team members with experience in relevant technologies. If lacking, invest in training for broader team capabilities.

### Support Resources
- Consider external resources or managed services for additional support, especially for complex systems like Kubernetes.

## 2. Migration Complexity
### Overview
Migrating to a new container orchestration platform involves several complexities:

- **Kubernetes**: Requires a significant architecture shift from Docker Compose to a declarative model using YAML manifests and encompasses complex configurations. 
- **Alternatives**: Some alternatives like Docker Swarm and Amazon ECS provide easier transitions with less required change in deployment practices.

### Steps in Migration
1. **Assessment**: Evaluate current applications to determine if they are stateless or stateful, impacting migration planning significantly.
2. **Preparation**: Optimize Docker images, ensure compatibility with the new platform, and prepare for the transition.
3. **Gradual Transition**: Incrementally migrate services to reduce risk by starting with non-critical applications.
4. **Monitoring & Adjustment**: Ongoing monitoring is crucial to adjust configurations based on performance metrics.

## 3. Cost Implications

### Alternative Solutions
